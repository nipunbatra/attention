Worked lessons for deep-learning students

Attention, vision and language

Start with the next token. Follow the same attention operation into translation, image patches, and answers grounded in an image.

Two connected series, with calculations you can inspect and diagrams you can build one step at a time. Read each lesson as an article or present the same content in class.

Attention and language

  1. PART 1

    From characters to next-token prediction

    Train a character model on names. Follow embedding lookup, a fixed context window, one hidden layer, and softmax. Then sample a name and see what temperature changes.

    For students who know MLPs, softmax and cross-entropy.

  2. PART 2

    Self-attention, from first principles

    Use the bank examples to work out queries, keys, and values. Calculate the scores, apply the causal mask, and mix the values. See how context changes the representation used to predict the next token.

    For students who finished Part 1, or who know embeddings, dot products and softmax.

    Read Part 2Present Part 2
  3. PART 3

    Learning, heads, and the Transformer

    Follow one error through the attention model and update its parameters. Then see how multiple heads, layer normalisation, feed-forward layers, and a stack of decoder blocks fit together.

    For students who finished Part 2.

    Read Part 3Present Part 3
  4. PART 4

    Cross-attention for translation

    Translate "the river bank" into "la rive". Follow a decoder query as it reads the encoder's keys and values, then use the retrieved information to predict the next French token.

    Builds on self-attention in Part 2 and Transformer blocks in Part 3.

Vision to language

Keep a small image in view as the task changes. Each part separates the worked toy from the full architecture and states what its numbers can, and cannot, demonstrate.

  1. VISION I

    An image becomes a sequence

    One block or two? Follow the pixels into patch embeddings, let CLS gather information, work out a prediction, and train the same model after it gets an image wrong.

    Builds on embeddings, attention, and residual updates.

    Read Vision IPresent Vision I
  2. VISION II

    Learn without class labels

    Hide patches and work out reconstruction error. Then compare MAE, DINO, and I-JEPA: where does each target come from, what gets predicted, and what gets updated?

    Exact loss calculators; clearly labelled illustrative teacher scores.

    Read Vision IIPresent Vision II
  3. VISION III

    Learn to match images and text

    Follow three pairs through encoders, normalization, all nine matching scores, and contrastive learning. Change the candidate descriptions and see why probability is not confidence.

    A trained CLIP-style toy with a reproducible parameter update.

    Read Vision IIIPresent Vision III
  4. VISION IV

    Use an image to generate an answer

    Connect the visual rows to a language decoder. Work through its mask, query, prediction, generation, and response loss. Compare a visual prefix with cross-attention.

    Two fitted image–answer pairs; explicit checks of visual evidence and training regressions.

Using these lessons

Each part is one self-contained page that works offline, including its diagrams and maths. The article and classroom presentation share the same content; there is no separate slide copy to keep in sync. Read the full explanation or switch to the lean article view.

Choose Present, or press P in an article. The slides have no permanent header or footer. Use ← and → to move through the builds, and open Controls or press C when you need navigation. Esc returns to reading. O opens the overview, S shows notes, and Presenter view opens notes and a clock in a second window.

The notation and object colours stay consistent across the lessons. Slide-PDF exports include revealed quiz answers by default, so they can be read without interactive controls.
