A wrong answer with a known label is a gradient waiting to happen. Train the attention matrices and the head, then look again at what CLS reads.

Learning from two labelled scenes

The model is wrong on scene B. Training changes stored numbers so that the mean loss over the two labelled scenes falls. We keep the patch projection, the position rows and the CLS start fixed, so the axis names stay true, and let Adam fit the four attention matrices and the class head for 1,500 steps. Every later number is the fitted model. Read the trained matrices as you read the starting ones: CLS now asks mostly “on the right?”, and keys now answer mostly with their column. The overlay before and after training is the picture to keep. Scene C, a mug the model never saw at that place, is the one test of what was learned.

logits = model(scenes)                      # [2, 2]: two scenes, two answers
loss = F.cross_entropy(logits, labels)      # mean over both scenes
The optimizer stores new matrices, not a better map for scene B: the map is recomputed from them on every pass.

for step in range(1500):
    optimizer.zero_grad()
    F.cross_entropy(model(scenes), labels).backward()
    optimizer.step()

Training changed six small matrices and one bias, and the model now answers both training scenes and the probe correctly. What it reads has changed with it: from the brightest patches to the right column. The next section looks at that reading for every row, not only CLS.
