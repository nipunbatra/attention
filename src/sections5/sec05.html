Routing: CLS chooses where to read

Bring the search roles of Part 2 to the image. CLS is the receiver: it asks with its query $\vq{q_0} = \ve{e_0} W_Q$. Every row, CLS included, publishes a key $\vk{k_j} = \ve{e_j} W_K$ that says when it is worth reading, and a value $\vv{v_j} = \ve{e_j} W_V$ that says what it would send. The matching axes have names too: bright region? and on the right? Before training the query asks both with weight one; a key answers the first with brightness and the second with column. Open a record, click a key in the routing tree, then look at the softmax weights painted on the image: that picture is what the rest of the page tracks.

CLS is the receiver. Its query says what it looks for: $\vq{q_0} = \ve{e_0} W_Q$. Each row publishes a key to match against: $\vk{k_j} = \ve{e_j} W_K$.

The key is a matching description. It is not the information CLS will receive; that is the value, in the next section.
