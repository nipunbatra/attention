E = E + attention(norm1(E))   # rows exchange information (sections 5 and 6, twelve heads)
E = E + mlp(norm2(E))         # each row is transformed on its own

LayerNorm before each sublayer keeps the rows at a common scale. The feed-forward sublayer transforms each row separately with two matrices and a non-linearity; it moves no information between rows.

Twelve heads read the same rows with twelve sets of $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ of width 64, and their twelve messages are concatenated before $W_O$. Twelve such blocks are stacked, a final LayerNorm is applied, and the class head reads the last CLS row.

The block follows the original Vision Transformer and its reference implementation; the sizes in the table are those of ViT-B/16. Vision II asks how such a model can learn without labels.

Everything we computed survives the change of size: patches become rows, each head routes and mixes independently, one output projection combines their messages, and CLS supplies the image classification row. The original ViT uses learned position embeddings, twelve heads in ViT-B/16, pre-normalized attention and MLP sublayers, and multiple blocks. Our toy's row and column position coordinates and its two-head worksheet are teaching choices.
