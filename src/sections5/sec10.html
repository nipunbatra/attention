Sixteen patches of four pixels became seventeen rows of four numbers. A real model changes every size and keeps every step.

Scale up to a real Vision Transformer

Return to the slider of section 1 with the whole chain in mind. A 224×224 photograph with 16×16 patches gives 196 patches and, with CLS, 197 rows, so one head compares 197 queries with 197 keys. Each patch has three colour channels, so its raw row has 768 numbers, and the projection maps them to a model width of a few hundred. The steps are the ones we computed: project, add position, prepend CLS, route, mix, project back, add, read CLS. A real block wraps attention in LayerNorm, follows it with a feed-forward sublayer, runs several heads and stacks a dozen blocks; those are named with their shapes and left out of the arithmetic on purpose.

$$\underbrace{r_j}_{1\times768}\;\underbrace{W_{\text{patch}}}_{768\times384}+\underbrace{b_{\text{patch}}}_{1\times384}=\underbrace{\ve{e_j}}_{1\times384}$$

E = E + attention(norm1(E))   # rows exchange information (sections 5 and 6, six heads)
E = E + mlp(norm2(E))         # each row is transformed on its own

LayerNorm before each sublayer keeps the rows at a common scale. The feed-forward sublayer transforms each row separately with two matrices and a non-linearity; it moves no information between rows.

Six heads read the same rows with six sets of $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ of width 64, and their six messages are concatenated before $W_O$. Twelve such blocks are stacked, a final LayerNorm is applied, and the class head reads the last CLS row.

The block follows the original Vision Transformer and its reference implementation; the sizes in the table are those of ViT-S/16. Vision II asks how such a model can learn without labels.

Everything we computed survives the change of size: patches become rows, rows are routed and mixed, CLS collects and a head reads. The extras of a real block, normalization, a feed-forward sublayer, heads and depth, are additions to that chain, not replacements.
