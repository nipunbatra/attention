One image has one label. We will follow its pixels to a class prediction.

Classify this image

The main calculation runs on one small model that you can check by hand: an 8×8 grayscale scene, sixteen 2×2 patches, one extra CLS row, one attention head with two matching axes, and a class head with two answers. We chose the patch projection and position rows so their axes have honest names, and we kept them fixed. The four attention matrices and class head were trained on two scenes. Section 10 separately replays the same image with a hand-chosen second head; those extra numbers were not trained.

  • It can show every number of a forward pass, what CLS reads before and after training, and how one update turns a wrong answer into a right one.
  • It cannot show what a real Vision Transformer learns: two training scenes fit a rule, and scene C in section 8 is the only test of that rule. A full ViT also has LayerNorm, a feed-forward sublayer, several heads and multiple blocks; section 10 distinguishes the two-head worksheet from a full pretrained model.
  • The photograph in section 2 illustrates the task. The model receives only the 8×8 scene shown here.

Part 2 gave every word a row and let the rows exchange information through attention. Here the input is an 8×8 grayscale image of a tabletop. Its label answers one question: is there a mug on the right half? The model receives the pixel values, not the words “mug” or “right”. We first make rows from patches, then reuse the attention chain from Part 2. Section 10 returns to the choice of patch size after the classification calculation is complete.

Scene A: two bright mugs, a book and a plant.

Ground-truth label: mug on the right. The other class is no mug on the right.

The pipeline of Part 2, unchanged after the first arrow

Only the way rows are made changes. Everything from $\ve{E}$ to $\vp{E'}$ is the operation of Part 2, run on seventeen rows instead of ten.
Two changes matter most: a projection instead of a lookup, and a new row, CLS, because an image has no last token to predict from.

A Vision Transformer decides how many rows an image becomes by choosing a patch side. From there on, the chain is the one we know. The next section shows where the fixed patch grid cuts the scene and how it supplies the model’s rows.
