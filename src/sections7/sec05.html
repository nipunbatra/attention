Each observed pair must be found from either side: image to text and text to image.

Train retrieval in both directions

The row softmax asks an image to choose its caption. Turn the question around and a caption must choose its image: the same logits, normalised down a column instead of across a row. Each direction has its own cross-entropy, the mean of $-\log p$ on the observed pairs. CLIP averages the two. The column version is not the transpose of the row version, because the denominators differ: a caption competes against the other images, an image against the other captions. Both losses send gradients into both encoders and into the temperature, so nothing in the matrix is trained from one side only.

$$\vd{L_{I \to T}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \log \va{p(T_i \mid I_i)}$$

$$\vd{L_{T \to I}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \log \va{p(I_j \mid T_j)}$$

A row asks which caption fits this image; a column asks which image fits this caption. Both must be right.

t = torch.arange(3)   # image i belongs to caption i
loss = 0.5 * (F.cross_entropy(logits, t) + F.cross_entropy(logits.T, t))
Rows and columns are supervised separately; neither direction solves a one-to-one assignment.

The contrastive loss is the mean of two cross-entropies: image to text over rows, text to image over columns. Both use the same logits with different normalisers, and both push the diagonal up and the rest down.
