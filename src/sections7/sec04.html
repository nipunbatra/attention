Each observed pair must be retrievable from either side.

Train retrieval in both directions

Image-to-text training asks each image to choose its observed caption. Text-to-image training asks each caption to choose its observed image. Both use the same logits, but they normalize over different candidates. Averaging the two losses trains both retrieval directions.

The target indices are $[0,1,2]$: row 1 wants caption 1, row 2 wants caption 2, and row 3 wants caption 3.

targets = torch.arange(3)
loss_image = F.cross_entropy(logits, targets)

$L_{I\to T}=-\frac13\sum_{i=1}^3\log p(T_i\mid I_i,\mathcal B)$

Competitor captions affect the denominator even though the target is one diagonal entry.

loss_text = F.cross_entropy(logits.T, targets)
# Each original column is now a candidate-image row.

For one bright dot, stripes and the dot initially tie at cosine $0.800$.

loss = 0.5 * (loss_image + loss_text)
# Both cross-entropies already average over the 3 examples.

Different normalizers give different losses in the two directions.

For a batch of $N$ pairs, the loss is $-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_i[\log p(T_i\mid I_i,\mathcal B)+\log p(I_i\mid T_i,\mathcal B)]$. Rows and columns are supervised separately; neither direction solves a global one-to-one assignment problem. During inference, two different images may legitimately retrieve the same description.
