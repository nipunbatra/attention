One dot product per pair fills a 3×3 matrix. A temperature and a softmax turn each row into probabilities.

Score every image against every caption

With six unit vectors in hand, compare every image with every caption, including the captions that belong to other images. The result is a 3×3 matrix of cosines: the row picks an image, the column picks a caption. It is a matrix of matches between whole examples, not an attention map between positions. Dividing by a temperature $\tau$ stretches the cosines into logits, and a softmax across a row asks one image to spread probability over the three captions on offer. The diagonal wins in every row at the start, but the book row's lead is thin: 0.75 against 0.61 for the plant caption.

$C_{11} = \ve{\hat g_1^{\mathrm{img}}} \cdot \vq{\hat g_1^{\mathrm{txt}}}$: scene A against its own caption

Both rows have length one, so the sum of products is the cosine of the angle between the two directions. It ranges from −1 to 1.

Images down the side, captions across the top: $C = \ve{\hat G^{\mathrm{img}}}\,(\vq{\hat G^{\mathrm{txt}}})^\top$

cosine = g_img @ g_txt.T   # [3, 3]; rows: images, columns: captions

$$S_{ij} = \frac{C_{ij}}{\tau}, \qquad \tau = e^{-a},\quad a \text{ learned}$$

logits = log_scale.exp() * cosine   # exp(log_scale) = 1 / tau

Scene A: $\va{p(T_j \mid I_1)} = \dfrac{e^{S_{1j}}}{\sum_{k} e^{S_{1k}}}$

Image to text: each row sums to 1

A probability here compares the three captions on offer. It says nothing about captions that were never written down.

Nine cosines, one temperature, and a softmax over each row: every image now holds a probability for every caption in the batch. The diagonal is what the observed pairs say should win.
