Three scenes, three captions. The joint coordinates get names before any number is chosen.

Three pairs and three named axes

The training data is three pairs from the shared scene: the two-mug scene with two mugs on a table, the book-only scene with a book on a table, and the plant-only scene with a small plant. Nine pairings are possible; three are observed and six are wrong. As in Part 2, we name the coordinates first. Both encoders will end in three numbers, and we call them mug, book and plant. On the text side the names are true by construction: the row for mugs starts as one on the mug axis and zero elsewhere. Function words such as a and on start at zero and must learn whatever helps.

The observed pairs. The model sees the 64 pixel values and the words.

Three observed pairs, six wrong pairings. The task is to score the observed ones above the wrong ones, in both directions.

The vocabulary and the word counts of the three captions

Order is dropped on purpose to keep the arithmetic visible. Words that never appear in a training caption keep their starting row.

The joint axes

The same three names label the columns of the image map. An image vector and a caption vector are comparable because they are written on the same axes.
