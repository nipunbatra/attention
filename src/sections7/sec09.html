Scope, questions and summaries

Before the summaries, the limits again in one place. Every number on this page came from a frozen image encoder trained for a different question, a 4×3 map, fourteen word rows and one temperature, fitted to three pairs. That is enough to see the mechanism exactly and nowhere near enough to claim anything about photographs. Answer the three questions out loud before opening them; each answer includes a computed table. The summaries at the end describe the same calculation as one sentence, as a chain of operations, and as equations, the way Part 2 did for attention.

  • The image side is Vision I's frozen encoder plus a 4×3 map. Its CLS rows lie on one line, so the images can only be spread along an arc; a richer encoder would use the whole sphere.
  • The text side ignores word order and knows 14 words. Words outside the three captions never trained.
  • Three pairs, 60 steps: the fit is memorisation. Pairs 4 to 8 and the one-mug scene are probes, not a test set.
  • A softmax over a candidate list compares that list. It is not confidence that a caption is true.
