One dot product becomes a matrix of candidate matches.

Compare every image with every caption

We have three image vectors and three text vectors. Compare every image with every caption, including captions from the other pairs. The cosine matrix $C$ is $3\times3$: its row chooses an image, and its column chooses a caption. It is a matrix of matches between whole examples, not an attention map between token positions.

$\ve{\hat g_1^{\mathrm{img}}}=(2/\sqrt5,1/\sqrt5,0),\quad\vq{\hat g_1^{\mathrm{txt}}}=(1,0,0)$

$C_{11}=(2/\sqrt5)\times1+(1/\sqrt5)\times0+0\times0$

$C_{11}=2/\sqrt5\approx0.894$

This is the two-square image matched against two bright squares. It is a similarity, not yet a probability.

cosine = g_img_unit @ g_txt_unit.T  # [3, 3]
# rows: images; columns: captions

Stripes also score $0.800$ against the dot caption: the encoders still confuse some pairs.

$S=C/\tau,\qquad\tau=0.5\quad\Rightarrow\quad S=2C$

logits = log_scale.exp() * cosine  # [3, 3]
# exp(log_scale) = 1 / temperature

The denominator contains all three candidate captions for this one image.

What changing temperature can and cannot do

For fixed vectors and a fixed candidate set, a positive temperature changes the sharpness of softmax but not the highest-scoring candidate. The ratio of two candidate probabilities is $\exp((C_a-C_b)/\tau)$. A cosine gap of $0.05$ at $\tau=0.01$ therefore gives a ratio of $e^5\approx148.4$, not a five-fold ratio.

The released CLIP implementation stores a learned logit_scale and exponentiates it. Its initialization is $\log(1/0.07)$; $0.07$ is the corresponding initial temperature. It is not a universal fixed temperature for every model or use case.
