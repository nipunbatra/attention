The image encoder now has language as a training partner.

A caption can supply supervision

The earlier parts built image representations from patches and from relationships between images. CLIP connects those representations to text. Its training example is an image together with the caption that accompanied it. The learning problem is to recognize those pairings among other images and captions in the batch.

Radford and colleagues trained CLIP on 400 million internet image-text pairs and evaluated transfer without training a new classifier on each target dataset. We will use three tiny pairs to inspect the objective. The original CLIP paper describes the full models and experiments.

What this toy replaces

The image branch is a learned linear map of 16 pixels. The text branch sums learned rows for words in an eleven-word vocabulary. There is no pretrained visual or language knowledge, no image augmentation, and no attention inside these toy encoders. The matching objective, unit normalization, and gradient update are computed exactly. This lets us examine the part of CLIP that connects the two modalities without repeating the full ViT calculation.
