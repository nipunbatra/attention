Why we are here: eight short questions test the distinctions that are easiest to blur when the full model appears at once.

Pause and think

Use these questions as a retrieval check rather than a reading checklist. Say an answer before opening its panel, then connect it to a specific equation or diagram from the lesson. Several questions protect distinctions that matter in implementation: parameters versus activations, full input rows versus projected head widths, attention across positions versus FFN mixing within a row, and causal masking during both training and generation. The last question returns to the layer example. A representation moving farther from its initial embedding proves that context changed it. Distance alone does not show that the new representation is more useful or more accurate.

Correct answers should name the mechanism, the direction of data flow, and the scope of the claim.
