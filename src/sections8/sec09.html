A fluent answer is not evidence. Two questions the scene cannot support, and what the toy does with them.

What the pixels cannot tell you

Grounding has two sides: the answer must follow the image, and the image must contain the evidence. The first probe changes one word of the question, mugs to book. The toy answers two again, with an identical overlay, because in this one-block decoder only the question mark's row asks, and that row does not depend on the word before it. The second probe cannot even be run: how heavy is the mug? has no answer in sixty-four brightness values, and no word for it in the vocabulary. When the input carries no evidence, a confident answer is a guess, however fluent.

Identical inputs give identical rows, weights and answers. When the evidence is not in the pixels, the right output is “not visible”.
  1. Change the image and keep the question. Did the answer follow? (Section 7: yes.)
  2. Paint the answer token's weights on the image. Did it read the object? (Section 5: the mugs.)
  3. Move the object. Did the answer survive? (Section 7: no, scene C says two.)
  4. Change the question. Did anything change? (Here: nothing.)

Reading the right patches, following the image and answering the question are three different checks. This toy passes the first two on its training scenes and fails the third and the moved-mug probe.
