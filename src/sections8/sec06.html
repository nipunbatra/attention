Freezing parameters does not mean cutting the gradient path through a frozen language model to a trainable connector.

The recorded warm-up fits two pairs with Adam. The displayed further step uses SGD at learning rate 0.02 so the parameter update is easy to state. The reference generator checks every trainable scalar by finite differences; students use autograd rather than deriving those gradients by hand.

LLaVA's original staged recipe first fits a connector with pretrained components frozen, then instruction-tunes with a different trainable set. This is a useful example, not a mandatory sequence of four stages for every VLM. Preference optimization can follow supervised training, but preference labels do not by themselves prove visual correctness. LoRA, full fine-tuning, frozen encoders, and joint training are choices whose value depends on data, domain, memory, and validation results.
