Sixteen patch rows and five word rows in one sequence; one attention block reads them; the head predicts the next token.

The pipeline, end to end

The whole part fits in one picture. The scene is cut into sixteen 2×2 patches. Vision I's encoder, with its trained attention frozen, turns them into sixteen rows of four named numbers. A connector maps each row to the decoder's width of three. The five words of the question become five more rows through a lookup table. All twenty-one rows enter the block from Part 2: queries, keys, values, one softmax per row, a message, an update, a residual addition. The question mark's updated row goes into the vocabulary head, and the tallest bar is two. Photograph this figure; every later section is a close-up of one of its parts.

Sixteen of the rows come from pixels, five from words. After that, attention does not care which is which.

The counts are scalars. Everything fitted was trained on two scene and answer pairs, then rounded to two decimals, so every table on this page can be recomputed by hand from the notation card.

Pixels become sixteen rows through the frozen encoder and the connector; words become rows through a lookup; the block treats all twenty-one alike. The rest of this part follows one row, the question mark, through that block.
