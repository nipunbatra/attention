The grounding test: change the image, keep the question

This is the payoff of the series. Keep the five words fixed and swap the picture. In scene A the first answer token reads both mugs and says two; in scene B it reads the one mug that is left and says one. Then a scene the model never saw: the single mug moved to the right. The model reads the moved mug and answers two. It did not learn to count; it learned that a bright mug on the right means two, which is exactly the question Vision I's frozen encoder was trained to answer. Two training scenes cannot tell those rules apart.

Hover a patch in either scene to see its weight.

The answer follows the mugs. Remove one and the first answer token reads the other and says one.

Reading the right patches is necessary for grounding; it is not sufficient. Scene C reads the mug and still counts wrong.

Painting the weights on the scene shows where the answer token looked. Changing the scene shows whether the answer followed. Scene C shows what two training examples leave undetermined.
