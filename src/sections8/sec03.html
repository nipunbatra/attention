A visual row has four named numbers; the decoder works with three. A connector changes the width, not the count.

Visual rows enter the decoder

Vision I ended with sixteen updated patch rows on the axes brightness, contrast, row and col; we keep them exactly, drop the CLS row, and freeze them. Notice that row and col are no longer plain positions: the encoder's update landed there, so those two numbers also carry the brightness a patch read from its neighbours. The connector multiplies every row by one 4×3 matrix and adds a bias. Its three output axes were learned, so we name each after the pixel quantity it tracks across the sixteen patches; the first tracks brightness almost exactly. The five words get rows from a lookup table plus a position vector.
