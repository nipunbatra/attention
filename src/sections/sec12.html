Why we are here: a small technical fix. The spread of raw dot products grows with the dimension, and dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ takes that growth out.

Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$?

This section is a short technical detour, and it exists because softmax is sensitive to the size of its inputs. A dot product over $d_k$ coordinates grows with $d_k$ even when nothing about the content changes, and a large gap between scores pushes almost all the weight onto one row. Move the $d_k$ slider and watch the two softmax columns: the raw one collapses onto a single row, the scaled one keeps the other rows alive. The second table shows the variance itself, from a simulation. The sentence to remember: dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ keeps the scores on the same scale whatever the dimension.

The score between a query and a key is a dot product over $d_k$ coordinates. Standard attention divides it by $\sqrt{d_k}$:

$$s_{ij} = \frac{\vq{q_i}^\top \vk{k_j}}{\sqrt{d_k}}$$

If the coordinates of $\vq{q}$ and $\vk{k}$ are roughly zero-mean and unit-variance, each product $q_l k_l$ has variance about 1, and the sum of $d_k$ of them has variance about $d_k$: $\operatorname{Var}(\vq{q}^\top \vk{k}) \propto d_k$. Bigger $d_k$ means bigger logits unless we rescale.
