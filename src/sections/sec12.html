Why we are here: a small technical fix. The spread of raw dot products grows with the dimension, and dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ takes that growth out.

Why divide by $\sqrt{d_k}$?

Softmax is sensitive to score differences. Under the independence assumptions below, the typical spread of a dot product grows like $\sqrt{d_k}$; not every individual dot product must grow. The first illustration holds four raw scores fixed and changes only the divisor. It shows what scaling does, not how score variance grows. A separate seeded simulation checks the variance argument, and the final table returns to our actual bank query.

The score between a query and a key is a dot product over $d_k$ coordinates. Standard attention divides it by $\sqrt{d_k}$:

$$s_{ij} = \frac{\vq{q_i}\cdot\vk{k_j}}{\sqrt{d_k}}$$

For intuition, assume all query and key coordinates are mutually independent, mean 0, variance 1. Then $\operatorname{Var}(q_l k_l)=1$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\vq{q}\cdot\vk{k})=d_k$. The standard deviation is $\sqrt{d_k}$.

Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ makes the variance 1 under those assumptions. Learned coordinates need not obey them exactly: this is a scale-control argument, not a promise that attention is always diffuse.

scores = (Q @ K.T) / math.sqrt(Q.shape[-1])
