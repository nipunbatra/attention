$$c_i = \sum_{j \le i} \va{\alpha_{ij}}\, \ve{e_j}, \qquad \sum_{j} \va{\alpha_{ij}} = 1$$

The weight $\va{\alpha_{ij}}$ says how much position $i$ takes from source $j$.

The raw weights are normalized to sum to 1.

You gave river more weight because it helps interpret bank. A model needs to choose those weights from its inputs. A different receiving token may need different information.

Where should the weights $\va{\alpha_{ij}}$ come from?

They should depend on what token $i$ currently needs and what information token $j$ contains. Both are functions of the representations themselves, $\ve{e_i}$ and $\ve{e_j}$. Attention is a way to build that function.

The weights determine the pooled vector. They should depend on both the receiving token and the sources it could read. A search example will help us separate those two roles.
