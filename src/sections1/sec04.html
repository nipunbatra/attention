Why we are here: characters are categorical symbols, while an MLP reads rows of numbers.

Characters to numbers

A one-hot vector identifies a character with one active coordinate. Different characters have zero dot product, so one-hot geometry offers no graded similarity. An embedding table replaces each long one-hot vector with a learned row of two numbers. Looking up a character means selecting its row; no averaging or calculation is needed. The first axis in this trained table was constrained so vowels are positive and consonants are negative. The second axis was free to move wherever the name-prediction loss found useful. These labels help us read the toy, but learned coordinates in larger models rarely have such simple names.
