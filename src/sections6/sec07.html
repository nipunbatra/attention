I-JEPA: predict a feature, not pixels

I-JEPA keeps the masked-image setup and replaces the target. A target encoder sees the whole image; its output rows at the hidden positions are the targets. A context encoder sees only the visible patches, and a predictor guesses the target rows from them. The loss is a squared error between representations, and the target encoder follows the context encoder by a moving average. Here both encoders are the frozen Vision I encoder, so the target for patch 8 is simply its output row on the content axes, and the predictor is the placeholder read of section 2 with a 4 × 2 head instead of a 4 × 4 one: 22 trained numbers.

with torch.no_grad(): target = target_encoder(all_rows)[hidden][:, :2]  # sg[y]
pred = predictor(context_encoder(rows[visible]), pos[hidden])           # 4-to-2 head
loss = (pred - target).square().mean()

What a feature target changes is the currency of the error. A pixel loss charges for every wrong intensity, including texture the context could never determine. A feature loss charges in the encoder's own terms: here two numbers per patch that Vision I gave names to. In the real method those numbers are learned and unnamed, and the target encoder keeps moving as the moving average of the context encoder; here it is frozen, so the target for a patch is fixed for the whole run.
