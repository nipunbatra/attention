Instead of hiding pixels, transform the whole scene twice and ask for the same answer.

Two views of one scene

A second family of targets never hides anything. Take one scene, make two views of it, a flip and a darker crop, and ask the model to give the same answer for both. No label is needed: the pair itself says "these belong together". The choice of transformations is the whole content of that request. A flip keeps "two mugs" but swaps left and right; dimming keeps the layout but changes every value. Our encoder is frozen, so the views' features do not move; what moves is a small head on top of them, and the third frame shows its two outputs being pulled together.

A three-slot head turns the summary row into a distribution; the loss asks the student's (view 1) to match the teacher's (view 2).

Agreement alone allows an empty answer: one distribution for every scene. DINO's recipe rules it out.

Notice what the two views share and what the request will therefore preserve. If the downstream question were "which side is the mug on?", a flip would be exactly the wrong view to ask agreement for. Augmentations are a statement about what the representation may ignore.
