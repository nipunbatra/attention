Same scene, same hidden patch, three different things to predict.

Three targets, one frozen encoder

Put the three methods side by side on the one patch we followed. MAE's target is the patch's four pixels, read off the image. DINO's target is a distribution over three slots, produced by a teacher head on another view of the whole scene. I-JEPA's target is the encoder's own row for the patch, two named numbers. All three targets are made from the image without a label, and all three are compared with something the model predicts from what it was shown. The table below also records which parameters received gradients in this toy and which followed the moving average, because that distinction is what keeps the two teacher methods from cheating.

The losses are not comparable across rows; they are the numbers each run reached in its own units, from the curves in sections 3, 6 and 7.

A self-made target must not be free to agree with the prediction. Pixels cannot move; a teacher moves only as a slow copy of the student.

The comparison is the map for the next part. CLIP keeps the two-branch shape of DINO but replaces the second view with a caption, so the target is no longer made from the image alone: it comes from text written by a person, and the two encoders learn to agree across that gap.
