Compute an error before talking about learning.

Which pixels enter the loss?

Suppose patch 4 is hidden. Its four true pixels are all 2. If the decoder guesses 1 for every pixel, each squared error is 1 and their mean is 1. We will use mean squared error per masked pixel throughout this exercise. A patch is either included in that loss or excluded; visible-patch errors do not enter the denominator.

Patch 4 is the lower-right patch. Its true pixel row is $(2,2,2,2)$.

$L=\frac{(1-2)^2+(1-2)^2+(1-2)^2+(1-2)^2}{4}=1$.

squared = (pred_pixels[masked] - patch_pixels[masked]).square()
loss = squared.mean()

This is an exact loss calculator, not a trained image reconstructor.

$L=\dfrac{1}{4|\mathcal M|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}\sum_{c=1}^{4}(\hat r_{jc}-r_{jc})^2$.

The loss trains the decoder and, through it, the visible-patch encoder.

Across images, a useful encoder can capture patterns that help predict missing regions.

Every value in the calculator follows the displayed formula. Each masked patch contributes four pixels. Its patch error is the sum of those four squared errors; the final loss divides the total by the number of masked pixels. A real MAE has a prediction vector for every masked patch, rather than our shared scalar guess.

The missing pixels may not be uniquely determined by the visible region. Reconstruction can encourage useful structure without reproducing every unpredictable detail. A low pretraining loss and a good downstream representation are related goals, not interchangeable measurements.
