Thirty-two numbers, one loss, a hundred steps. Then the reason the mug comes back grey.

Train the decoder: steps 0, 10 and 100

Training changes the decoder's 32 numbers and nothing else: the encoder stays as Vision I left it. The loss is the mean squared error over the hidden pixels, averaged over three training scenes with the same four patches hidden: A (two mugs), B (one mug) and D (book only). Press the step buttons to move along the curve and to redraw the prediction. Then look at the last frame. Scenes A and B have the same visible patches, so the decoder must give one answer for two different holes. The best single answer under a squared error is between them, and that is the grey mug.

$$L = \frac{1}{4\,|\mathcal M|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}\sum_{c=1}^{4}\big(\hat r_{jc} - r_{jc}\big)^2 \qquad \mathcal M = \{3, 4, 7, 8\}$$

Pixels are a poor target when the visible part does not decide them.

The training loss stops near , not at zero, and the split by scene says why: A and B share one prediction, so their errors cannot both vanish. A loss curve alone would not tell you that; the per-scene table and the two-originals picture do.
