An encoder can learn from an image even when its class label is missing.

The image supplies a training target

In Part I, we turned an image into patch rows and passed them through a Transformer. A classifier can learn from those rows when we have labelled images. But a large image collection may have no class labels. Self-supervised learning constructs a training target from each image itself: hidden pixels, a teacher's output on another view, or a representation of a hidden region. These are different objectives, even when they use a similar ViT encoder.

Visible patches are inputs. Hidden pixels supply the target.

The visible region may suggest a mug handle or a book edge, but it need not determine every hidden pixel. A reconstruction objective asks the model to make a prediction from that context and compares it with the withheld original. Across many images, this task can train useful visual representations.

We are reusing the idea of an encoder, rather than continuing the classifier's saved training run. The objective now asks for information from the image instead of a class label. The drawing uses four patches so we can see every input. Real pretraining uses many images and usually many more patches. Its objective does not by itself prove that the representation will work for recognition, segmentation, or another downstream task; that requires evaluation.

We will compare three target choices: MAE reconstructs pixels, DINO matches a teacher's probability distribution across views, and I-JEPA predicts target-region features.
