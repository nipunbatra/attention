DINO: a teacher supplies the target

DINO has a student and a teacher with the same architecture. The student learns by gradient descent; the teacher is a slow moving average of the student and receives no gradient. The student sees one view and outputs a distribution over some slots; the teacher sees the other view and produces the target distribution, after subtracting a running mean of its own logits (centring) and dividing by a smaller temperature (sharpening). Here the encoder is frozen and the two "networks" are two 4 × 3 heads on the CLS row, trained on scenes A, B, D and E with two views each. The numbers in every table come from that run.

Both heads read the frozen encoder's CLS row $\vp{e_0'}$. The student's logits are $z_s = \vp{e_0'}W_\theta + b_\theta$; the teacher's are $z_t = \vp{e_0'}W_\phi + b_\phi$ on the other view.

target = ((z_teacher - center) / tau_t).softmax(-1).detach()  # no gradient
loss = -(target * (z_student / tau_s).log_softmax(-1)).sum(-1).mean()
teacher = 0.9 * teacher + 0.1 * student                        # after the step

Centring stops one slot from winning everywhere; sharpening stops the output from going flat. Without the first the loss falls to 0; without the second it sits at $\log 3 = 1.099$. Neither number means the encoder learned anything.

The real DINO uses many more slots, a teacher momentum near 1 and temperatures near 0.1 and 0.05, and it trains the whole encoder on many crops per image. The two collapses it guards against are the ones on this page, and the paper shows them the same way: switch a part of the recipe off and watch the teacher's entropy go to zero or to log K (Caron et al. 2021, section 5.3).
