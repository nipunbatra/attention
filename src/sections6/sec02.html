MAE: hide patches, predict the pixels

A masked autoencoder hides patches, runs the encoder on the visible ones only, and lets a small decoder predict the hidden pixels. The loss compares the prediction with the pixels that were hidden. Our version keeps Vision I's encoder frozen and trains only the decoder: for every hidden slot a placeholder row asks the visible patches a question, reads them through the same keys and values the encoder already computes, and turns four numbers into four pixels. That is 32 trained numbers. The frames below follow the slot of patch 8, the centre of the right mug, and show the decoder after training; the next section trains it.

The decoder never sees the right mug and draws a faint one from what the visible patches say.

Dropped, not blanked: attention runs among the 13 rows that remain, with Vision I's $W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O$.

rows = patches @ W_patch + pos          # 16 patch rows, plus CLS
encoded = encoder(rows[visible])        # 13 rows in, 13 rows out

The query, written out: one line per key axis, each entry of $u_8$ times the matching column of $W_Q^{\mathrm{dec}}$.

The full read for the slot of patch 8, all twelve visible rows. The weight column is the attention row of the placeholder's query against the visible keys; the values are the encoder's, unchanged. The footer is the weighted sum the decoder actually receives.

The same four lines as a worksheet: the decoder row times each column of $W_{\mathrm{dec}}$, then the bias.

z = torch.cat([message, pos[hidden, 2:]], dim=-1)   # what it read, where it is
pred_pixels = z @ W_dec + b_dec                       # 4 numbers per hidden slot

Real MAE hides about 75% of the patches and trains a Transformer decoder with several layers together with the encoder. What survives from that design here is the sequence of decisions: drop the hidden rows, add a learned placeholder plus position for each hidden slot, read the visible rows, predict pixels, and score only the hole.
