A second view can supply a different kind of target.

Learn from two views of one image

Instead of asking for missing pixels, we can ask two transformed views of an image to produce related representations. For an object-recognition task, a moderate crop or a colour change might preserve the information we want. This gives us a positive pair without a class label. The choice of transformations matters: agreement across an augmentation teaches the model to ignore some changes, including changes that may matter to another task.

These are actual transformations of our four-patch image, not outputs from a trained model.

We can ask an encoder to retain information shared across both views.

If every image produces the same vector, its two views agree perfectly.

Contrastive objectives also compare against other images. DINO uses a teacher and a different anti-collapse recipe.

Agreement need not mean that every intermediate patch row becomes identical across views. A method may apply its loss to a particular global output or to a separate projection head. The backbone still produces the representation used downstream.

The contrastive drawing is a schematic explanation of the positive/negative distinction, not a simulation of training. We will now work through DINO's alternative: a student matches a slowly changing teacher distribution. The teacher's targets and the training recipe are what make this more than matching two arbitrary random vectors.
