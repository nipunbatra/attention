The calculator from the loss formula, fed by the trained decoder instead of a guess you type.

Which pixels enter the loss?

Every value here follows the formula of section 3. Choose a scene and a training step; the table lists the four hidden patches, the true pixels, the decoder's four numbers and the sum of squared errors per patch. The footer divides the total by the number of hidden pixels, sixteen, and that quotient is the loss the optimizer saw for this scene. The visible pixels never enter: they are copied into the drawing so you can see the whole scene, but the decoder is not asked about them and is not scored on them.

squared = (pred_pixels[hidden] - pixels[hidden]).square()   # [4 patches, 4 pixels]
loss = squared.mean()                                        # divide by 16, not 64
Only the hole is scored. Visible patches are inputs; hidden patches are targets; no pixel is both.

The denominator is a design choice with a meaning: it makes the loss a per-hidden-pixel error, comparable across masks of different sizes. The real MAE also normalises each patch's pixels before comparing; our pixels are already on one small scale, so we compare them directly.
