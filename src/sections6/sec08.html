What did the encoder learn? A linear probe

Pretraining gives you an encoder, not a promise. The standard test is a linear probe: freeze the encoder, pool its output rows into one feature per image, and fit a line with labels for a task it was never trained on. Our task is "two mugs or one mug?". The feature is the mean of the sixteen patch rows on brightness and contrast, so the probe is a line in that plane. Twelve views of three scenes give twelve points: A (two mugs) against B and C (one mug), each as the scene itself, flipped, dimmed, and cropped. The crops are held out.

two mugs (A) one mug (B, C) held-out crops. Dashed: $w\cdot f + b = 0$; shaded: "two mugs".

The probe found an axis, brightness, that counts bright pixels rather than mugs.

Read the probe as a measurement, not a verdict. It reports one line, on two named axes, fitted to nine points. The failure on the crops is informative in the same way the moved mug was in Vision I: it names the shortcut. Real probes use thousands of held-out images and features with hundreds of unnamed axes, and they report accuracy without being able to draw the line.
