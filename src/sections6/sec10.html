Summaries and pause-and-think

Try to explain the part without the page: one sentence, one chain of operations, three losses. The sentence is what you would say in a corridor; the chain is what you would draw on a board; the losses are what you would write in an exam. Then answer the four questions before opening them. 3 · Mathematical

$\mathcal M$ is the hidden set, $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ the stop-gradient, and in DINO the teacher parameters follow $\phi \leftarrow \beta\phi + (1-\beta)\theta$ instead of the gradient.

New since Vision I: the hidden set $\mathcal M$; the placeholder $u_j = m + p_j$, its question $\vq{q_j}$ and its read $g_j$; the decoder row $z_j$ and the two predictions $\hat r_j$ (pixels) and $\hat y_j$ (feature); the DINO logits $z_s, z_t$, centre $c$, temperatures $\tau_s, \tau_t$ and distributions $p_s, p_t$; the moving average $\phi \leftarrow \beta\phi + (1-\beta)\theta$ and the stop-gradient $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$; the pooled feature $f$ of the probe.
Every target in this part came from the image itself. Vision III takes the target from a caption, so the encoder learns to agree with words.

Three targets, one encoder, and one probe with labels at the end to say what the features kept. When you read a pretraining paper, find those four things first: what is hidden or transformed, what is predicted, what is compared, and how the result was measured.
