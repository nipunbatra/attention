## What must be fixed

### 1. Vision II stacks a new table on every slider event, so the visible table is stale and contradicts the loss line on the same slide — **blocker**

`src/sections6/sec05.html:41` and `src/sections6/sec03.html:~41` both call `AT.ui.table(..., {into: …})` inside `draw()`. `put()` in `src/shared.js:277` only ever `appendChild`s — it never clears the host — so every `input` and `change` event appends another table. One real mouse drag on the s05 DINO cross-entropy slider leaves 13–26 stacked tables and grows the section from ~2100px to ~3975px. The table a student actually sees is the *topmost*, i.e. the one for `a = 0` (p_s = 0.2119 / 0.5761 / 0.2119), while the slider reads 3.00 and the aria-live loss sentence beneath it correctly reads 0.5243. Two contradictory answers on one frame, in the frame whose entire teaching point is that changing the logit moves the student distribution toward the teacher. In present mode it also raises the author-only banner *"Authoring check — frame 05.3 is 2337px too tall"* and a dashed red outline on the projected slide (s03 shows the same at 1749px). **Fix:** build the figure once outside `draw()` and call `fig.update(rows)` inside it (`ui.table` already returns a handle with `update`, `shared.js:~902`); or, minimally, `S.querySelector('#s05-ce-table').replaceChildren()` as the first statement of `draw()`, same for `#s03-lab-table`. While there, give `put()` a `replace` option or document the append semantics in CONTRACT.md so the next author does not step in it.

### 2. Part 3 tells students a finite-difference check matched the analytic gradient exactly — **major**

`src/sections3/sec04.html:107` renders *"2 entries were checked by finite differences. Maximum absolute error: 0.00e+0."* The cause is `src/train_part3.py`: `main()` runs the whole training dict through `rounded()` (`round(value, 4)`), which destroys the true error before it reaches `src/toy3.json`, where `abs_error` and `max_abs_error` are stored as `0.0`. The real error is not zero — `python3 src/check_training.py` measures 4.51e-11 at `tok_emb[bank][0]` over 260 gradient scalars. This is the opposite of the lesson in a section that is literally teaching gradient checking: finite differences always leave truncation and round-off error, and a numerate student will call this out from the floor. **Fix:** exclude `finite_difference` from the `rounded()` pass (or store the error at full precision), regenerate toy3.json, rebuild part3.html. Better: report the stronger evidence you already have — "260 gradient entries checked by central differences at ε = 1e-5; maximum absolute error 4.5e-11" — but keep the quoted ε and method consistent with the number shown (the stored check uses ε = 1e-4, `check_training.py` uses central differences at 1e-5).

### 3. Part 3 prints the position table as 10 × 5; it is 20 × 5 — **major**

`src/sections3/sec03.html:42` builds the row from `T = AT.train.sentence().length`, so the live page renders *"position table | 10 | 5 | 10 | 5"* under a caption asserting that each parameter's gradient has exactly the same shape as the parameter. The stored parameter is 20 × 5 (`AT.model.pos_emb.length` = 20, `toy3.json max_context: 20`), and so is its gradient. It contradicts your own Part 3 §13 ("The toy has twenty position vectors"), and it teaches the classic misconception that the position table is sized by the current sequence length. The token row (20 × 5 = |V| × d_model) is correct and needs no change. **Fix:** `const nPos = AT.model.pos_emb.length;` and use `[nPos, d, nPos, d]`, relabelling the row "position table (max context × d_model)". One line.

Leave the per-layout cell sizes alone — those are not a defect.

### 5. Present mode: the arithmetic popover lands off-slide, and it triggers the author-only warning banner in front of the class — **major**

`placeBelow()` (`src/shared.js:472-479`) reads offsets from `getBoundingClientRect()` — post-transform screen pixels — and assigns them to `style.top`/`left` on an element inside the present stage, which is CSS-scaled by `--present-scale`. Reading mode is fine (dy = +6). In present mode the displacement grows with the scale factor: at 1280×720 dx = −408; at 1920×1080 dx = +226 and the popover occupies x = 1742…2660 while the stage ends at 1877; at 2560×1440 it lands at x = 3485, entirely off the viewport. Its overflow then raises `#at-fit-warning` — at `?present#s05/4/0` on a 1080p projector the slide shows the yellow *"Authoring check — frame 05.4 is 76px too tall and 686px too wide"* banner and a red dashed outline that were empty before the click. This kills the click-a-cell-to-see-the-arithmetic interaction, which is the central live affordance of Part 2. **Fix:** convert screen deltas to stage coordinates — `var s = fig.offsetWidth ? fr.width / fig.offsetWidth : 1;` then `top = (ar.bottom − fr.top)/s + 6`, `left = (ar.left − fr.left)/s`, `minLeft = (12 − fr.left)/s`, `maxLeft = (vw − 12 − fr.left)/s − w` (`el.offsetWidth` is already stage px). Re-run the present walk with hover + click and confirm `#at-fit-warning` stays empty.

### 7. Present mode: touching a slider on stage kills the navigation keys — **major**

`shared.js:2091` bails out of `handleKey` for any focused `INPUT` (`inField`, `shared.js:2074`, is true for every input type). Verified in headless present mode: with the range focused on attention.html s03, four ArrowRight presses leave `AT.present.state()` at `{fi:11, build:0}`; clicking empty background restores it immediately. Or blur the range on `change` while `AT.present.isActive()`.

## Assessment

**What this does better than almost anything else on the subject.** The numbers are real and they are guarded. Every checker in the repo passes — `toy_ref`, `check-live-model` (8220 values to 1.3e-15), `check_position_capacity` (84,968 values), `check_training.py`, `train_part4/vision3/vision4 --check`, all four `check_visionN.mjs` at 1e-11 gradient error, `frame_audit` and `check_tables` on all eight pages — and `check_vision4.mjs` actually rebuilds Vision I's encoder from toy5.json and asserts Vision IV's frozen front end reproduces it row by row, which is more cross-part integrity than most published courseware attempts. The `=` versus `≈` logic in `ui.matVecCalc` is a small piece of intellectual honesty that almost nobody bothers with. The row-vector convention holds without a lapse across four parts; the causal-mask treatment is exactly right, including the sharp observation that a row summing to 1 is not evidence against leakage; the √d_k section states its independence assumptions and backs the variance claim with a seeded simulation rather than an assertion. And the scoping discipline is the best thing here: "It demonstrates content routing, not word-order sensitivity"; "a conditioning check, not a generalization test"; "It proves the updated parameters fit that example better. It does not establish generalisation." Part 3 §06 openly shows the mean-loss step making position 10 *worse*. Material that tells students what its own toy cannot show is rare, and it is what makes this safe to put in front of a class. The slide architecture is real too — 430 hand-authored frames, zero legacy auto-frames, 947 present states walked with no errors and no overflow, a working two-window presenter over `file://`, and a 16:9 PDF exporter.

**The single most important thing to fix next.** The two Vision II calculators that stack tables instead of updating them. It is a one-line fix in each file, and until it lands, the two frames whose entire purpose is *watch this number move* show a student a frozen table that contradicts the loss sentence directly beneath it — and, in present mode, print an author-only overflow warning across the projected slide while doing it. Nothing else in this review is both that cheap and that damaging. Fix it, then the Part 3 gradient-check zero, then the position-table shape — those three are the only places in eight parts where a student can walk away holding something false.
