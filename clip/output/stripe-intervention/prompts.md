# Generated stripe intervention

Built-in OpenAI image tool, 29 September 2026. One original and one edit; both are retained. The four labels and scale 100 were fixed in `plan.json` before generation/inference. This follow-up was selected after the toucan edit failed to produce a large class-share change; the bird outcomes remain in the lecture and evidence.

## Original — figures/zebra-stripes-original.png

Use case: scientific-educational. Generate ONE square realistic wildlife photograph-style teaching illustration. A single adult plains zebra with clearly visible black and white stripes, entire body in frame in a clean side profile facing right, standing still on short green-brown grass. All four legs, hooves, tail, upright mane, face, ears and muzzle are visible. The zebra fills most of the frame with modest margins. Background: a simple softly blurred grassy plain with no other animals, fences or buildings; daylight, plausible anatomy, sharp fur texture. Keep the image composition simple, suitable for a subsequent controlled edit to remove the stripe pattern while preserving the animal's silhouette. No text, labels, watermarks, borders, arrows or collage.

## Scope and attribution

These are generated teaching illustrations, not sourced wildlife photographs. The intended edit removes the dark stripe pattern while approximately preserving the rest of the scene. Local contrast, apparent coat colour and incidental pixels can also change. This is not a biological change of species or a direct intervention on a model concept.

Encoding and matching follow [Radford et al., CLIP, 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/radford21a.html). The later concept-level intervention preview cites [Koh et al., 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/koh20a.html). This particular experiment, candidate menu and illustrations are newly authored.
